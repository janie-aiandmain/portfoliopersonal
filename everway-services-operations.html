Scaling a National Professional Services Model

At Everway, I inherited a service operation with avoidable manual work, weak service-consumption controls, inconsistent packaging, and limited structured feedback. I rebuilt the operating system around repeatability, product adoption, and service economics.

The operating problem

Routine engagements depended too heavily on human discovery, scheduling, preparation, and institutional memory. Salesforce did not reliably connect purchased services with delivered services, creating the risk of repeated uncompensated delivery—including costly onsite engagements.

What I wanted to change

Create a service catalog that Sales could confidently sell, give facilitators repeatable experiences to deliver, preserve custom scoping for genuinely advanced needs, and make service consumption visible enough to protect both customer experience and margin.

What changed

Service catalogDefined SKUs and “shoppable” agendas for new and intermediate customers.
Salesforce controlsConnected purchased services to delivery so consumption and entitlement were visible.
Workflow automationReduced human time spent scheduling sessions and preparing facilitators.
Survey systemCreated team goals around survey capture and closed-loop customer feedback.
Product feedbackShared recurring customer sentiment, friction, and feature needs from delivery sessions.

How success was measured

Product utilization was measured through actual customer behavior—not attendance alone—including educator and student logins, assignment activity, and lesson creation. Service economics included client revenue against delivery costs such as labor allocation, travel, and the technology stack.
