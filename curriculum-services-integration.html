Integrating Specialized Services After Acquisition

After Instructure acquired a curriculum review/alignment service, I helped integrate the team into Professional Services and replace costly, meeting-heavy scoping with a repeatable commercial and delivery model.

Leadership scope

At different points I led the asynchronous services development team, instructional design team, and curriculum alignment team. The curriculum service arrived with its own ways of scoping, communicating, and estimating work, so integration required more than moving an org chart box.

The old scoping model

Sales would bring Services into multiple discovery meetings. Curriculum SMEs could spend paid hourly time reviewing potential work before the engagement was even sold. Quotes varied based on who scoped the project and how much review happened before a SOW existed.

The design question

How do we keep expert judgment for complex work without paying expert rates to rediscover the same scoping logic on every opportunity?

The redesigned model

Standard intakeGather the inputs required to understand curriculum type, complexity, and quantity.
Rules-based estimateAuto-calculate cost using repeatable complexity and volume factors.
Standard SOWUse common scope language, assumptions, and deliverables.
Exception pathEscalate genuinely complex opportunities for expert custom scoping.

Why it mattered

The change reduced scoping effort and cost, improved quote consistency, created clearer client communication, and made the acquired service easier for the broader Professional Services organization to sell and deliver.
